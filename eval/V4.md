# v4 — what Gold 200 records (the default app since 4.0; index.html?v3 = v3)

Goal (user, v4 scope): take the DNG at the phone's exposure, impress it on Gold 200, and show what the film recorded as a
positive with a neutral inversion: no paper, no scanner look, no light sliders. Colour, shadows, midtones, highlights and
the transitions come from the film; highlights must not clip.

## Pipeline
- Exposure: the phone's (engine meter on the thumb, as v3 Auto). Nothing else touches the light before the film: the v3
  highlight dodging (Luci auto) is off (`DODGE` in app.js) and Texture / lens (CA, vignette, falloff) start at 0.
- Film: the engine's Gold 200 (spectral sensitivities, density curves, DIR couplers, halation, grain).
- `scanner.model = "narrowband"` (engine narrowband.rs): the engine returns the developed negative read in three narrow
  bands (625 / 522 / 455 nm, FWHM 20 nm unverified), transmittance with the orange mask, as an RGB-LED scanner or a
  densitometer reads it. Until v4 prototype 2 it was `null`, the legacy colorimetric scan (CIE observer under the viewing
  illuminant): its broad overlapping bands mix the dyes, and the inverted colours came out flat and shifted (below).
- Inversion (web/lib/invert.js, GPU output pass in output.js, CPU twin). Since 3.48 the scan has the film-type setup (engine
  NarrowbandParams.setup, app.js NARROWBAND): each channel's base-removed band density is mapped, per level of the stock's own
  grey ramp (0.5 EV steps over +-6 EV), onto G's, so a grey the film recorded is equal in the three channels and the mask is gone.
  - Black / white: one for the three channels, the frame's thinnest / densest channel (0.5 % / 99.8 %): nothing clips.
  - Mid grey: the frame's median density lands on display mid grey (0.18).
  - In between, log-linear (density is log exposure): the film's toe and shoulder are the only shaping.

  Before 3.48 black / white were per channel from the frame, which also removed any overall cast but stretched the channel with
  the least range:
  - a sunset (IDG_20261022_182651_466) got a cyan sky the DNG does not have: blue share of the upper sky 69 % vs the DNG's 19 %,
    relative chroma 0.44 vs 0.19;
  - the white barn wall of the gold200 dataset came out cyan.

  With the setup both follow the DNG. The film's own rendering stays: Gold reads a blue sky deeper than its RGB, and a rock in
  blue shade 0.7 EV bluer than red where the DNG has 0.5. The phone's white balance is the only colour correction, as on a
  daylight film with no lab correction.

  The real negatives of the gold200 reference keep the per-channel inversion (eval/gold200/invert_scan.mjs: their stock's grey
  axis is unknown; inverted on our engine's axis their lights turn cyan, the stocks differ there).
- Magenta/Verde, Giallo/Blu: shift the G / B channel between its black and white (0.003 of the range per step).
- Soft ends: past the white / black anchors (percentiles) the curve approaches white and BLACK/8 asymptotically instead of
  clipping (IG export of the sea DNG: fully clipped whites 0.132 % -> 0.004 %).
- Output: always sRGB (photos end on the web / Instagram). The engine renders Rec.2020 and the output pass maps it into sRGB
  with a soft per-channel gamut compression (output.js GAMUT.srgb, thr 0.8 / lim 1.6: covers the Rec.2020 cyan, the farthest
  colour) and, for a pixel whose largest channel is still over 1 (the matrix pushes bright warm tones past 1 in R), a pull
  towards its own luminance until that channel is 1 (luminance and hue kept; neutrals and whites untouched, white stays 255),
  instead of the hard clip of an engine sRGB render; v3 keeps Display P3 for P3 photos. An earlier roll-off of the peak
  above 0.9 also dimmed neutral whites (white 251): replaced.
- Degenerate frames: the mid-grey anchor is kept in 10-90 % of the range and a channel narrower than 0.05 density gets that
  range (a half-black or half-white frame put the anchor on an end: division by zero, colour noise).

## Scanner tab
Nessuno (default): the narrowband scan + neutral inversion above, the film alone. Frontier: the Frontier SP-3000 model on the same
negative (v3's scanner with its Gold-look corrections; its AutoSetup measured on demand, per photo). Magenta ↔ Verde / Giallo ↔ Blu
are the scanner's colour keys (the inversion's or the Frontier's cmy), so they live in this tab. Switching back and forth is
pixel-identical (measured). The choice is remembered (fcf_scanner).

Hyper Tone (Frontier only, slider 0 = off, user choice: manual, no auto): the Frontier's local tone control, in the scanner
stage. Engine params.rs HyperToneParams: each pixel's film density moves along the stock's own grey ramp (density per EV
measured on the ramp at 0.18 x 2^±0.5) by -amount x map(x, y) EV, before the scan LUT; the map (web/lib/hypertone.js) is a
self-guided-filter base of the thumb's log2 luminance (radius 1/10 of the frame, edges above 1 EV kept), EV from its median,
512 px long side, set once per photo (Engine.set_hyper_tone_map), sampled bilinearly in frame coordinates (export tiles read
the same map). Slider 1 = 0.5 of the map (HYPER_MAX, a choice). First version (map from the frame's median, no dead zone)
flattened whole photos (user): now a recovery of the extremes: the map is EV from mid grey as the film is exposed (a mostly
dark frame has its median in the dark and its shadows were left alone), areas within ±1 EV of mid grey untouched
(DEAD_EV), and the lift is the scanner's (the same density on the three layers), not more light. Where the negative holds
no information (G dye density below the stock's toe end, 0.215 from the datasheet curves) the red layer gets up to 18 %
less of a lift (its toe slope 0.283 vs G 0.346; engine stock_toe / hyper_apply): lifted voids go cool, as on the machine
(user). Measured at 100: rock shadows L* 11.5 -> 23.6, mids 50.6 -> 54.9, lights 90.4 -> 88.3; interior mids unchanged;
rock at -1.7 EV, deepest half L* 3.7 -> 8.6, a*/b* +0.3/+1.2 -> -1.7/+2.7 (a dark slate teal-green, not full cyan).
A 96 px map left a light rim on a rock against the sky (a step spread over ~20 px); 512 px removes it (measured by eye, zoom).
Our Frontier model has none of the real machine's defects (sensor floor at density 3.3, never reached by Gold; no grain-frying
sharpening; no 8-bit), so "migliorato" is the default by construction; "Fedele" (adding them back) is postponed (user).
AutoSetup robust to one-colour scenes (v4 Frontier, app.js V4_NEUTRAL_SIGMA 0.06; engine FrontierAutoParams.neutral_sigma,
0 = the old trimmed mean, v3): the colour key is read from a mean weighted 1 / (1 + (chroma / 0.06)^2) in setup density, so
a lawn or the sea barely counts and a real cast (which moves the greys too) is still read. Green park ORF Y key -0.065 ->
-0.018 (the grey path no longer pink), tungsten cat C +0.035 -> +0.024, neutral frames within 0.01.
Hyper Sharpness (Frontier only, slider 0 = off): the engine's scanner unsharp mask, radius 12 µm on the film (~one Frontier
scanner pixel, a choice), gated by relative detail at that radius (ScannerParams.unsharp_threshold, engine unsharp_gate /
unsharp_combine.wgsl): t0 / t1 0.03 / 0.08 at the preview pitch (measured on the v4 Frontier rock, grain 3.1x: flat-sky grain
p99 0.032, rock structure p50 0.085), scaled with the pixel pitch (as Nitidezza) and x2 per 2 stops below mid grey up to x4
(shadow grain passed a fixed gate: dark rock looked fried). One gain on the three channels (no colour fringes). Measured
(mean fine detail, slider 1): sky grain x1.00-1.01, rock structure x1.43 preview / x1.58 export.
Lab operator's keys (Scanner tab, Frontier and Noritsu; app.js labKeys / labModel): the SP-3000 manual's controls (PP3-B1271E2):
Densità (density key, -9..+9, + = denser / darker as on the machine), Gradazione (Gradation Selection Soft3..Hard2), Tono luci /
Tono ombre (Tone Adjustment highlight / shadow Soft / Hard), Saturazione (-9..+9), Bright (Bright Mode colour intensity -2..+4).
The manual gives the levels, not the step sizes: 1/4 EV per density step, ~14 % mid-tone slope per gradation level, highlight
key +-0.5, black lift +-0.15 (engine black_lift now -1..1: Hard shadows clip the deepest tones), 5 % saturation per step, +8 % per
Bright step on the green / cyan / blue / magenta hue knots (skin and greys untouched, as Fujifilm describes it): all choices.
Why they matter: on a dark backlit frame (sunset, IDG_20261022_182651_466) AutoSetup lifts ~2 EV, as the real Frontier lifts a
dark frame, and the sky between the clouds loses its blue; the operator's answer is density (D +4: the blue comes back) and,
since the Hyper Tone fix (map referenced to the scanned level, not the film's), Hyper Tone luci.
Noritsu (Scanner tab, app.js V4_NORITSU / V4_NORITSU_AUTO): the Frontier's scanner model with a Noritsu's tone (a 9.26, white
1.0), per-hue colour, warm-shadow balance and a highlight-anchored AutoSetup, fitted on the same Color Precision frames scanned
on their Noritsu (eval/frontier_cp, "Noritsu"): chart dE00 6.4-6.7 -> 4.3-4.5 over -1..+2 EV; no Hyper Tone / Hyper Sharpness
(the Frontier's).
Frontier tone and colour against a real Frontier (app.js V4_FRONTIER, method and numbers in eval/frontier_cp): Color Precision's
Gold 200 chart frames (ColorChecker SG + Kodak grey scale, -1..+2 EV, the same negatives on a Frontier and a Noritsu), rendered by
our v4 Frontier with the operator's density / C M Y keys as free nuisances. The guessed v3 curve (a 6.2) was too soft: both real
machines fit a 9.3-9.6, no shoulder (start 0.95, sharpness 0), black 0.023, white 0.97 (grey L* rms 2.6 -> 1.2: deeper lower mids
and blacks). The real Frontier is much less saturated than ours (chroma 0.80 of the chart's, Noritsu 0.90, ours 1.12): saturation
1.01 -> 0.84, saturation_blue 0.15 -> 0.05; hue rotations confirmed (blue 22, green 20 deg). Chart dE00 7.2 -> 6.6 (0 EV), held-out
-1 / +1 / +2 EV 7.5 / 7.2 / 7.6 -> 6.0 / 6.4 / 6.5. In the app: sea / rock mean L* 53.1 / 48.8 -> 51.4 / 47.0 (shadows firmer).
With the knee left at 0.69 AutoSetup's highlight cap darkened whole frames (rock sky grey, mean L* -11): hence start 0.95.
Step 2 (v4): saturated greens / cyans were 5-11 L* lighter than real (vivid greens 21): a Frontier-stage darkening of saturated
green-cyans (opponent hue ~112-214 by relative chroma, chromaticity kept; Fujifilm's 3D LUT handles "highly saturated
cyan/green"; now part of `hue_lightness`, step 4) 0.46, saturation 0.92, hue rotations 18 / 20: dE00 0 / -1 / +1 / +2 EV 6.6 / 6.0 / 6.4 / 6.5 -> 5.7 / 4.8 / 5.2 / 5.4;
film-side causes (DIR, green-layer flank) and a 559 nm green sensor band were tried and dropped (eval/frontier_cp).
Step 3 (v4): chroma by hue, engine `hue_saturation` (6 knots: R Y G C B M 0.88 / 1.06 / 0.97 / 1.04 / 0.73 / 0.64): dE00 5.3 /
4.5 / 4.8 / 5.1, chroma 1.05 of the real. AutoSetup colour strength from the chart's tungsten frame: the real Frontier keeps about
half the cast our 0.3 kept under a simulated 3200 K and takes the red out: k_col 0.68 (app.js V4_AUTO, a lower bound: the
light's temperature is unknown).
AutoSetup density (v4, app.js V4_AUTO): the scans' Lightroom settings are all 0, so levels are the lab's. Full exposure
compensation (strength 1.0: the real mid grey stays within 2.4 L* over -1..+2 EV). A first fit on the grey-wall chart frame
alone (target 0.16) made outdoor photos washed out (user); on scenes rebuilt from the real scans the lab's mid grey is 67 / 80 /
53 (wall / black studio / outdoors), ours 58 / 86 / 59 at v3's level (75 / 86 / 76 at 0.16). Since 4.1 the key is read 60 % from
the bright large areas (engine `highlight_weight` 0.6, target_offset -0.12): 62 / 79 / 57. Checked on all 20 Gold Frontier frames
of the tool (5 scenes x 4 EV, `eval/frontier_cp/recon_model.py`, Landscape and Night not in the fit): level error rms 0.57 ->
0.29 EV (harbour -0.79 -> -0.04, night +0.80 -> -0.03; the outdoor portrait +0.21 -> +0.41, still too bright).
Step 4 (v4): `cyan_green_darken` became `hue_lightness` (per-hue light of saturated colours, 6 knots: 0.91 / 0.92 / 0.46 / 0.75 /
0.90 / 0.96), plus `hue_shift` (3 / 1 / 6 / 7 / -5 / 12 deg): dE00 4.4 / 4.0 / 3.9 / 4.1 (0 / -1 / +1 / +2 EV), colours' lightness
+0.1 L* of the real (blues +4.8 -> +1.8).
Open: cyan hue ~18 deg greener than real.

## Measured (browser, ?v4)
Sea DNG sky: L* mean 79.8, p95 94.0, std 8.5 (v3 Frontier 82.7 / 92.9 / 7.8). Densities used by the frame: about 0.5–1.3
(R), 0.8–1.7 (G), 1.2–2.2 (B) of the scan, i.e. the film's straight line: these scenes barely reach toe or shoulder.

## Tried and dropped (kept here, not in code)
- Film conversion from shot data with a single-use camera (f/10, 1/125): indoor −1.1 / −4.2 EV, sun +2.4…+3.1 EV.
  A compact (f/3.5, 1/30) exposes all the Indigo DNGs correctly. Dropped: exposure stays the phone's for now.
- Optical print on Portra Endura + a lab colour analyser (enlarger Y/M from two probe prints, k 0.7): works (brightness
  kept by print_exposure_compensation), but the paper flattens skies (sun +2.4 EV: sky std 5.9). Dropped: no paper.
- Teal shadows do not come out of film + paper by themselves: in v3 they were the Frontier model's toe parameter.
- Engine calibration inputs (camera EV, enlarger filters) are ignored by spektrafilm-wasm `Engine.update`: changing
  them needs a new Engine.
- Regression check: eval/check/cases.json has four ?v4 cases (v4_sea, v4_rock, v4_cat, v4_orf, `"query": "?v4"`). An earlier
  note said render.cjs could not render ?v4: that was the calibration-input bug above, not the script.

## Against real Gold 200 (eval/gold200, three scenes)
Reference: the gold200 dataset's film scanned with RGB LEDs (the film's own record, no scanner look) and inverted with the
same neutral inversion; v4 on the same scenes' digital raws (converted to DNG). Framing differs (handheld), so these are
global statistics. Chroma p50 v4 / film:
- colorimetric scan (v4 prototype 1): 0.42 / 0.61 / 0.75 (barn / sunflowers / gladiolus);
- narrowband scan (now): 0.55 / 0.73 / 0.92. On the same registered pixels, with anchors from those pixels: 0.84 / 0.88.

First calibration step (v4 only, app.js V4_DIR): DIR inhibition same-layer / inter-layer 0.6 / 0.6 -> 1.0 / 1.0 (they had no
Gold data behind them, eval/calib/CALIB.md). Registered chroma 0.84 / 0.88 -> 0.91 / 0.97; in the app, global chroma 0.55 / 0.73
/ 0.92 -> 0.64 / 0.85 / 1.04. 1.2 / 1.2 overshoots the film's strongest colours (C p90 61 vs 55). Not the cause of the red
deficit below: spectral upsampling (hanatos2025 / mallett2019, adaptation surface), band peaks ±5 nm, FWHM 20-35 nm and
Rec.2020-primary bands all leave it within ±0.5 of mean |da|,|db|. Calibration continues as references come in.

Second calibration step (v4, app.js V4_STOCK; engine FilmRenderingParams.layer_speed_ev, applied to the profile's density
curves when the engine is built): red layer +0.75 EV. Method: per scene, the film exposure is a nuisance fitted first (the
film camera's meter differs from the phone's): the engine is rendered at -1.5 ... +1 EV (sf-eval, CPU, same v4 params) and the
per-channel normalised-density distributions of the registered pixels are compared quantile by quantile (QQ: robust to the
handheld misregistration that spoiled the per-pixel fits). Best: barn -0.5 EV (QQ 0.035 of the range), sunflowers -2 EV
(0.019). At that exposure the tone curve already matches (blacks, shadows, mids, contrast within 2-4 % of the range): the
more open v4 shadows of the first comparison were the exposure, not the film. Per channel, red alone wanted ~+0.5 ... +1 EV
more than green / blue: red speed 0 / 0.5 / 0.75 / 1 EV -> mean |a*| over tones and both scenes 8.6 / - / 4.2 / 3.6, hue
error +14/+8 / +9/+5 / +7/+2 / +4/-1 deg; 1 EV turns the lights red (a* +5.9). In the app at the phone exposure: barn mids a*
-12.6 -> -3.4, gladiolus -10.3 -> -6.7 (sunflowers stay ~-15: the 2 EV exposure gap). Saturation at the fitted exposure is
mixed (0.76-0.90 barn, 1.19-1.31 sunflowers): DIR left as it is. Scripts: eval/gold200/cpu (sf-eval trial, registration, QQ).

Still open (measured, not fixed): v4 shadows and mids are greener / less red than the film (a* -7…-17 in L* 0-75,
b* -6…-12 in the mids of two scenes), highlights match; the film is warmer in the mids. Per channel, the film's red density
sits above green / blue in the mids at the same engine density (frame 13: 0.51 vs 0.38 / 0.40 at 0.45 of the range):
the engine's red-layer curve shape, or its spectral input, differs from the real stock. DIR couplers x2-3 move it little
(mean |da|,|db| 7.1 -> 6.5). Registration is handheld-limited (r 0.7-0.85 after smoothing): enough for a direction, not
for a per-channel fit.
