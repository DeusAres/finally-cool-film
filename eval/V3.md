# v3 debug — automatic highlights (debugv3.html → index.html?v3)

Goal: only Esposizione + Contrasto; highlights handled automatically, keeping cloud modelling and detail.

## Why a curve alone cannot do it
A per-pixel tone curve that keeps a bright cloud's internal contrast must flatten every tone at that level;
with more contrast the scanner shoulder starts earlier and clouds go flat/white (or, with the v2 highlight lock,
pale/hue-shifted). Minilabs solve it spatially: Fuji's digital dodging (patents US 6,987,589 / US 6,700,685 /
US 6,807,316: low-frequency density compressed, detail untouched; Frontier "Hyper Tone" is reportedly this,
second-hand), the darkroom's burning-in of skies.

## Model (web/lib/dodge.js, INPUT_WGSL_V3 in web/lib/lens-gpu.js)
- Base B: self-guided filter of log2 luma of the AutoSetup thumbnail (256 px long side), radius 8 % of the
  long side, eps 1.0 (EV²). Frame-referenced, so preview, export tiles and any resolution share it.
- Shift in the input pass, scene light before the exposure gain: `s = A (1 - exp(-rho e / A))`,
  `e = max(b - knee, 0)`, `b = max(B(pos), own log2 luma)`. Slope <= rho = 0.8 < 1: no tone reversal (no halo or
  rim at skylines: a plain ramp gave a bright rim, measured), constant shift inside bright areas (modelling kept).
- knee = median(B) + 1.0 EV (sweep, 8 DNGs: same highlight gain as +0.5, mid contrast kept at 0.96 instead of 0.86 at Contrasto +1); A solved so the 99th percentile of B lands at median + head(contrast),
  head = 2.6 - 1.2 c (c >= 0), 2.6 - 1.5 c (c < 0): more contrast, earlier shoulder, more dodging.
- AutoSetup and the exposure meter still see the undodged thumbnail (dodging them too made AutoSetup
  brighten the whole frame back, measured).
- Contrast: `frontier.debug_contrast_mode = 2` (rational density remap about mid grey, ends fixed; no lock).
